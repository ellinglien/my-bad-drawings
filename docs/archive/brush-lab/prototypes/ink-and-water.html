Ink and Water

Life that is real ink-and-water physics. A line drawn into a wash that is still wet lets its ink out into the water, where it feathers and curls, then sets as the wash dries. Inside a drying wash, pigment drifts, clumps and settles into the paper's valleys. Nothing grows on dry paper.

How it works
  • Plumes. Where a line crosses wet paint, part of its ink (more the wetter) leaves the line as parcels. They ride the water's currents, spreading and paling as they go, branch now and then, and stain the paper as they pass. They stop at the water's edge and leave a faint tide line there. On dry paper the line is untouched.
  • Drift follows the currents; Vein adds Living Ink's mould senses, so parcels follow each other's trails into channels and reach for other lines nearby.
  • Settle. A wash lays part of its pigment at once; the rest stays suspended. While wet it drifts, clumps (flocculates), rolls into the paper's valleys and, late, out to the drying edge, and drops onto the paper faster as the water goes.
  • 10 fixed ticks a wet second; speed falls with wetness, so it all slows and stops. Then it is fixed for good.
  • Only darkens: everything multiplies onto the page by a darkness that only grows.
