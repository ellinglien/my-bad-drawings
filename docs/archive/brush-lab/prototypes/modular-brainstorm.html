Modular Brush Ideas

How other modular systems make plugging parts together understandable, and three UI concepts for Inkwash's Brush Lab. Grayscale, no animation, Manta 1024×1365 and Nomad 749×998 dp portrait.

Short answer

The systems people learn fastest have a fixed path that already works (semi-modular synth, camera, pen), one module in focus at a time with few controls, and a live result beside the controls. Free graphs (cables, nodes) are where people get lost.

Recommendation: A, the Semi-modular pen. Six fixed parts read top to bottom from hand to page. Tap one to see every alternative drawn with your brush, and open its dials only in Advanced.

23 systems, as the user meets them

Each card covers what you touch, why it's learnable and what goes wrong.

Eurorack / VCV Rack

graph

Touch Panels of jacks and knobs. Every control is visible. You add a module from a browser and drag cables between jacks.

Learnable You can hear every change at once. Players adopt a layout habit of control at top-left and sound out at bottom-right (ModularGrid).

Trouble An empty rack makes no sound. Beginners are told to copy a known patch first (VCV community). Cables pile up into spaghetti.

Semi-modular synths (Mother-32, 0-Coast)

chain + override

Touch A fixed panel. The jacks are pre-wired ("normalled"), and a cable overrides one link.

Learnable "No patching required" (Moog via XLR8R). The 0-Coast "operate[s] with or without the use of patch cables" (manual). It makes sound the moment you play it.

Trouble Normals are invisible: plugging one jack can silently mute something else (ModularGrid).

littleBits

chain

Touch Magnetic bits snap in a line, colour-coded by role.

Learnable The magnets "enforce polarity, so you can't put things in the wrong way" (we-make-money-not-art). A circuit works the moment it snaps.

Trouble Expression is limited. The coding is colour only, so it needs translating for grey.

OP-1 / Pocket Operators

slots

Touch Four colour-coded knobs, matched to the same colours on screen (TE guide). Pocket Operators have only two knobs.

Learnable "Always four controls" per page. Designer: when you turn a knob "you should hear the difference" (Pamono).

Trouble A knob's meaning changes with the page.

Elektron parameter locks

per-step override

Touch Hold a step and turn a knob. The locked value shows inverted and the step flashes (Elektronauts).

Learnable A lock is one gesture, and it is local.

Trouble You can't see which settings are locked without paging through them (Elektronauts).

Ableton Racks

macro

Touch Eight macro knobs on the front. Each maps to many settings, each with its own min/max (Sound On Sound).

Learnable One knob makes one musical move. Shared racks open to their macros.

Trouble The macro hides what changes underneath.

Guitar pedalboard

chain

Touch A row of boxes, each with two to four knobs and a bypass footswitch.

Learnable Each pedal does one thing. Bypass gives an instant A/B. There is a well-known conventional order (Guitar Player).

Trouble The order changes the sound (MusicRadar), and beginners agonise over it.

Line 6 Helix

chain on screen

Touch Select a block in the chain, turn to change its model, press for the model list. Its knobs edit that block (cheat sheet).

Learnable Select-then-swap: one block in focus.

Trouble Users call the flow view "engineer-brain-centric" (The Gear Forum).

MicroFreak mod matrix

grid

Touch A 5×7 grid of sources (envelope, LFO, press…) against targets. Pick a cell and set an amount (Sound On Sound).

Learnable There are no cables. The whole routing is one small table.

Trouble Its three assignable columns hide their targets.

Node editors (Blender, Nuke, TouchDesigner)

graph

Touch Boxes and wires on an infinite canvas. A group folds nodes into one box and exposes chosen inputs (Blender manual).

Learnable Groups hide complexity. Expert-made groups give beginners a few knobs.

Trouble Blank-canvas paralysis and spaghetti. Only experts build from scratch.

Photoshop Layer Style

fixed list

Touch A checklist of effects on the left. Tick to enable, click the name to see its settings on the right. Since CS6 the list order is the order they are applied (Tuts+).

Learnable Fixed order and plain names. Every effect is visible but off by default.

Trouble You can't reorder, and users asked for it for years (Adobe community).

Procreate Brush Studio

slots

Touch An attribute list (Stroke path, Taper, Shape, Grain, Wet mix…), that attribute's settings, and a Drawing Pad that redraws your test strokes "in real time" (handbook).

Learnable Sections follow the anatomy of a stroke. The preview never leaves the screen.

Trouble There are 12–14 sections of sliders, so it is power-user territory.

Krita / MyPaint

slots + curves

Touch Pick an engine from a list, which starts a fresh preset (Krita docs). Each option has sensors (pressure, tilt, speed…) with curves (Krita sensors). There is a scratchpad.

Learnable Deep and exact.

Trouble Swapping the engine throws away your settings. Several sensors on one option give "weird results" (KDE bug 497367). Curves are opaque.

iOS Shortcuts

vertical chain

Touch Action cards stacked top to bottom. Each output flows to the next automatically (Apple).

Learnable Reading order is the flow, so no wires are needed.

Trouble Long shortcuts scroll out of sight.

Scratch

shape-keyed blocks

Touch Blocks whose shapes only fit where they are valid (K-State).

Learnable Shape prevents most syntax errors, with no colour needed (arXiv).

Trouble Semantic mistakes remain.

Noita wands

slot chain

Touch A wand has N slots, and spells cast left to right (Noita wiki).

Learnable It is visible and ordered, and you edit in a safe room.

Trouble Emergent combinations are opaque, so players need the wiki.

Zelda TotK Fuse/Ultrahand

free attach

Touch Grab and stick. The parts look like real objects, so you can guess what they do (Zelda Dungeon / GMTK).

Learnable Staff tuned the combinations "so results wouldn't disappoint" (Nintendo, Ask the Developer). A visible "glue" shows what is attached.

Trouble Free 3D building is fiddly with a controller.

Spore Creature Creator

snap to body

Touch Seven part categories. Parts snap only to valid spots, and more columns unlock as you go (SporeWiki).

Learnable Reviewers placed it "right down the middle" of simple and advanced (Giant Bomb).

Trouble The large catalogue needs sorting (Newsweek).

Minecraft redstone

free graph

Touch Dust wires in the world.

Learnable Physical and visible.

Trouble Hidden rules ("quasi-connectivity") break machines that "feel like they ought to work" (Minecraft feedback).

Cameras

slots

Touch Body, lens, film, filter. The mount decides what fits (PictureCorrect). Fujifilm "recipes" are a handful of named settings shared as text (Fuji X Weekly).

Learnable Each slot is a physical object with a name. A recipe is shareable.

Trouble Mount incompatibility.

Fountain pen

slots

Touch Nib, ink, paper. A wet nib on absorbent paper feathers (Pure Pens).

Learnable Everyone already knows the model, and the interactions are physical.

Cooking / coffee

elements

Touch Salt, fat, acid, heat as a "compass" rather than a recipe (NPR). The coffee compass steers by outcome: sour, weak, bitter, strong (Bean Scene).

Learnable A few named elements, and dials named by what you perceive.

LEGO / Unix pipes

one connector

Touch Studs fit everything since 1958 (LEGO). Pipes pass text between small programs that "do one thing well" (Wikipedia).

Learnable One universal interface.

Synplant, rejected before, fits the evidence: its radial garden (MusicRadar) has no physical counterpart, and Synplant 2 added a conventional "DNA editor" beside it (CDM).

12 patterns that make modular understandable

  1. 1
    Normalled defaults ("always a mark").

    From: Semi-modular, littleBits, cameras.

    Every module always holds something: Life = None and Carry = Off are real choices, not empty sockets. Any combination draws, so there is never a broken or blank state.

  2. 2
    A fixed order that reads as the flow.

    From: Pedalboard, Photoshop styles, Shortcuts, Noita.

    The modules sit in one order: Hand → Tip → Medium → Carry → Paper → Life. That is the order things happen when you draw. There is no reordering, because the physics has one order.

  3. 3
    No free graph.

    From: Lesson from the failures of Eurorack, node editors and redstone.

    No cables, no canvas and no hidden normals. What is shown is what is connected.

  4. 4
    Select, then swap.

    From: Helix, Procreate, Photoshop.

    Tap a module, and its choices replace the strip under the test area. One module is in focus; the rest stay put.

  5. 5
    Choices drawn with your brush.

    From: Procreate Drawing Pad, the Lab's variations.

    Each choice card is the current brush with only that module changed. This is WYSIWYG and static, so it works on e-ink.

  6. 6
    Few controls per module, with one macro on top.

    From: OP-1 (four), Pocket Operator (two), Ableton macros.

    In Simple, each module has one dial named by its result, as the coffee compass does: Tip thin↔broad, Medium dry↔wet (the existing Wet dial), Paper smooth↔rough, Life still↔lively, Hand steady↔responsive.

  7. 7
    Role coding without colour.

    From: littleBits colours become Scratch shapes.

    Each role gets a fixed position, a fill pattern on its tab (solid, hatched, grey, lined, dotted, open) and a glyph. Position does most of the work, and the pattern links a module's card, its choices and its dial page.

  8. 8
    Constraints instead of errors.

    From: littleBits magnets, Scratch shapes, Spore snapping, camera mounts.

    A choice that would do nothing is greyed, not hidden, as v2 already does with nib width on a round nib. Better still, each choice adapts so it always does something, as TotK tuned its combinations.

  9. 9
    Locks at module level, clearly shown.

    From: Elektron's weak spot.

    A padlock on a module card keeps that whole part through Randomize, for example "keep my paper, roll the rest". Per-dial locks stay inside the module, and a locked module card shows inverted.

  10. 10
    Presets as recipes.

    From: Fuji recipes, Salt Fat Acid Heat, Racks.

    A brush is the five choices in words, such as "Split nib · Wash, wet · Cold press · Drying · Press→wider". The plain description and share codes already fit this.

  11. 11
    Input as a small matrix, not curves.

    From: MicroFreak, against Krita's stacked sensors.

    Pressure, tilt and speed form the rows; size, darkness and width/angle form the columns. Each cell is off, some or full. One source per target avoids Krita's "weird results".

  12. 12
    Bypass to learn.

    From: Pedal footswitch, Photoshop checkbox.

    Holding a module card draws the test loop with that module at its neutral choice, so you see what it adds. This is a static before/after, with no animation.

A

Semi-modular pen Recommended

A fixed column of parts, hand to page

Analogies: Semi-modular synth (a pre-wired path that works untouched) · pen and camera (named physical parts) · Line 6 Helix (select, then swap) · Photoshop Layer Style (fixed list, settings beside). Keyed joints borrowed from Scratch/littleBits.

  • Main. Five module cards plus a slim Carry card in physical order. Each card shows its pattern tab, the choice in words, a sample of what it adds and, in Simple, one macro slider (thin↔broad, dry↔wet, smooth↔rough, still↔lively).
  • Swap. Tap a card: the area under the test area becomes "TIP: pick one", a grid of every choice drawn with your brush with only the tip changed. Tap one to apply it (Undo reverts), ✕ to go back.
  • Dials. In Advanced the tapped card opens in place; the others shrink to one-line rows. It shows choice chips and every dial with a lock and dice. One card is open at a time, so it never scrolls.
  • Simple vs Advanced. The same column. Simple: choice plus one macro, no locks, with STYLE ▾, Randomize and "This brush" under it. Advanced: module padlocks on the cards and full dials.
  • Randomize · locks · variations. Randomize rolls every unlocked module, and a padlock keeps a whole part ("keep my paper, roll the rest"). Variations nudge each module, and at most one module's choice changes per card.

Strengths

  • The most conventional: a list beside a canvas.
  • The physical order teaches the model.
  • Swaps preview in place, reusing v2's "strip that swaps".

Risks

  • Nomad: a 260 dp column is tight for sliders, so it widens to 300 dp when a card is open (test area 449 dp).
  • Six cards fit 998 dp only if "This brush" stays at about three lines.
B

Pedalboard

A chain of boxes you can bypass

Analogies: Guitar pedalboard · Line 6 Helix · Ableton Rack macros.

  • Main. A full-width test area, then a board of six 112 dp boxes joined by short cables. Each box has a name, a model, one macro bar and, on optional effects (Carry, Paper, Life), a bypass footswitch with an LED.
  • Swap. Tap a box's name: a Helix-style model list covers the test area, one row per choice with a sample and a line of description.
  • Dials. Tap a box body: an edit panel covers the board, with tabs for every box, choice chips and two columns of dials with locks and dice.
  • Simple vs Advanced. Simple: one bar per box, with the library behind LIB. Advanced: lock pads on the boxes and the edit panel.
  • Randomize · locks · variations. As in A. Bypass adds a quick "with and without" lesson.

Strengths

  • Bypass is a very clear learning tool.
  • A wide test area.
  • A metaphor Elling knows well.

Risks

  • Six boxes use the full 749 dp; a seventh module (Knife, Finger) won't fit.
  • A chain suggests you can reorder it, and you can't.
  • Tip and Medium can't be bypassed, which breaks the metaphor.
  • The library becomes a second screen.
C

Recipe card

The brush as named ingredients

Analogies: Fujifilm film recipes · Salt Fat Acid Heat · iOS Shortcuts · nib + ink + paper.

  • Main. The test area on top and a recipe card below it. Each line reads as a sentence ("TIP Split nib ▾"), and the plain description closes the card.
  • Swap. Tap a line: a picker opens under it, each row a choice name, a short description and a sample drawn with your brush.
  • Dials. In Advanced, ▾ opens the line into its dials as an accordion, one at a time, with a pin per line.
  • Simple vs Advanced. Simple: lines and pickers only. Advanced: pins (module locks) and dials.
  • Randomize · locks · variations. "Surprise me" rerolls the unpinned lines. Variations are "other recipes", captioned with the line that changed.

Strengths

  • Words first, and the easiest to learn.
  • Ideal for sharing ("post your recipe").
  • Fits any width.

Risks

  • The least like a playground: close to today's Advanced list in sentences.
  • On the Nomad the test area drops to about 360 dp (260 dp in Advanced).

Pick: A, the Semi-modular pen, with two borrowings

  • Conventional. A list beside a canvas, like Photoshop's Layer Style, Procreate's Brush Studio and today's lab. Only the six words are new.
  • Physical. A pen is already modular (hand, tip, ink, paper, time), and the column reads in that order, so it teaches itself.
  • Always a mark. Every slot is normalled and there are no cables, so spaghetti, blank racks and hidden normals can't happen.
  • A playground, not weird. Tap a part to see every alternative drawn with your brush; lock a part and roll the rest. It keeps Randomize, locks and variations.
  • It fits the Nomad without scrolling, and v2's "strip that swaps" carries the picker.
  • From B, bypass. Hold a module card and the corner sample shows the brush with that part neutral: the pedal's lesson without the reorder promise.
  • From C, words. Each card says its choice in plain words, and "This brush" is the lines joined, so the recipe is the share text.

Open questions

  • Should Carry be its own slim card, or a dial inside Medium?
  • Should Input sit at the top as "Hand", or at the bottom as "How you hold it"?
  • Should a variation be allowed to swap a module's choice, or only nudge its dials?
