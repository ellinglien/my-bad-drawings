A · Line + wash

  • The pen engine lays the line, the paint engine the wash, from the same samples.
  • Wet moves ink from the line into the wash, which bleeds it; the wash grows from a halo to a full body and takes the nib's shape and angle.
  • Line: how much crisp line is left at Wet 1.
  • Wet 0 and Wet 1 are today's engines to the bit, so old brushes don't change.

B · Wet nib

  • One nib. Wet swells its water past the nib and lets the pigment run through it, gathering at the water's edge.
  • Reads as one fat wet mark, not a line over a wash.
  • Wet 1 is not today's paint, so old paint codes need the old engine kept beside it.

Merged dials

  • Grain = paper tooth + granulation
  • Bristle = dry brush + brush streaks
  • Pool = pooling + hollow edge
  • Each pair has one member per kind, so old codes load without loss.

Lines

  • Lines sit inside the wash's footprint, so they widen, taper and turn with it: one mark.
  • Centre: today's line. Spread: 2–5 lines across the wash, along the nib angle. Outline: ink on the wash's two edges, broken where it thins. Squiggle: one line meandering within it. Split: dip-pen tines parting.
  • Breathe: pressure opens them, a light touch brings them together; 0 holds them fixed.
  • The lines bleed into the wash with Wet, and the wash darkens along them. B draws a centre line only.

Offset · Chaos · Mixing

  • Offset: the line drifts off the wash, past its edge, and starts or ends before or after it.
  • Chaos: spacing, width, ink and place wander on seeded noise; lines break and restart; the squiggle loses its rhythm.
  • Mixing: the line goes grey and fuzzy in heavy wet wash and stays crisp on dry paper. The wash pulls branching feathers of ink from it, and pools where the line crosses its edge.
