Marble and Bristle

Marble: a scripted bath (32 drops in three sites, a swirl, a comb pass), then a print. Bristle: one 900 px stroke at 16, 32 and 64 bristles. Each runs twice to check it reproduces.

How it works
  • Marble: rings are vector outlines. A drop pushes every point out by √(r² + R²) (Jaffer and Lu), so area is kept exactly.
  • Comb and swirl: each tine is a moving stylus with a divergence-free (stream function) flow, so the rings deform smoothly and keep their area.
  • Ink and Clear alternate after each drop, as in suminagashi. Hold the pen down to let a drop keep spreading.
  • While you swirl, only ring outlines are drawn. When you lift, you see the fill. Set prints it (or it prints 8 s after your last touch).
  • Bristle: each hair has a home in the brush head. A spring pulls it home (Cohesion) and paired sensors ahead read the others' fading trails (Fluoddity). The hairs move toward trails or away from them, so strands split and rejoin.
  • Dryness 0 never runs out; the load curve goes with Dryness cubed, so only high Dryness runs dry quickly. Fast strokes skip a little more. Each stroke starts with a full brush.
  • Bristle steps come from pen travel only, 1 per px, so a stroke is final at lift and repeats exactly from seed plus samples.
