# Speed and clean-code audit (2026-10-19, branch `inkwash-audit`)

The owner asked for "an audit of the code and … optimizations … approaches simplified, things merged, etc. Focus is
on speed for the user, but I do want clean code". His biggest complaint: big brushes (3 mm and up: washes, hake,
bristle, stump) are sluggish on the Manta. Strokes take a while to appear, and the finish after lift is slow.

This branch is `inkwash-v2-lab` at `7bb0b1d` (brush names, magnets and notebooks merged) plus small commits. Every commit keeps
these the same:

- the bench's 210 page hashes, at 8, 1 and 1000 samples a present, and with every stroke quick;
- `test_inkwash` (72 PASS) and `test_document` (111 PASS), including LegacyConversionChecks and the goldens;
- Brush Studio: 115 of 115 checks, and 604 of 604 codes read and written back the same;
- `:app:assembleRelease` builds.

Nothing the user sees has changed except speed.

**How it was measured.** The desktop is an M2 Max. "Manta" means the desktop number ×20, as the engine spec found for
Java. That is an upper bound for native code: zlib, memcpy and the native compositor run about ×5–10.

The scratch tools are in `scratchpad/audit-tools/`, outside the repo:
- `AuditProf` emulates the Manta: a 4-core JVM fed the bench's 1.5 s stroke 20× faster than real time, with the quick
  render's compose-ahead scaled the same way. Every wait it reports, ×20, is a Manta estimate.
- `WriteBench` times the page write at lift.
- `GrainBench` and `NoiseBench` time the grain and paper-noise tables.
- `jfrtop.sh` reads JFR profiles at 1 ms.
- `guards.sh` runs every guard.

## Where the time goes for a big brush (the top complaint)

A big brush's stroke is quick: a flat preview under the pen, the true stroke rendered on its own thread, then written
at lift.

**Pen-down is cheap.**
- `QuickStroke.start` plus the first two samples costs 0.04–0.55 ms on the desktop, so at most about 11 ms on the
  Manta.
- The pen's thread spends 0.003–0.018 ms per sample on the preview, so 0.06–0.36 ms on the Manta.
- What the owner sees under the pen is the panel: one gray update, about 270 ms, after the brush's edge reaches a
  pixel. Add the present spacing (1.5 × the driver call, 8–100 ms). This is the "panel's side" finding in the engine
  spec, and the fast update can't be used because of the ghosting.

**The true render mostly keeps up.** Emulated with 4 cores, the render had drawn every sample by lift for every heavy
preset at its Small, Medium and Large size (719–720 of 720). Its share of the stroke's time:

| Preset | Share of the stroke's time |
|---|---|
| Wet hake 360 | about 74% |
| Hog bristle 120 | about 73% |
| Bristle brush 24 | about 85% |

These are the ones nearest to falling behind. On the device the recovery save took a core from them as well (fix 2).

**After lift, the critical path is the page write.**
- The render composes ahead every 100 ms, so after lift only the newest bit is composed (0.0–0.7 ms on 4 cores).
  Then the pen's thread writes the stroke's tones to the page (`StrokeTones.applyTo` → `ToneDocument.tones`).
- That write is 1–4.6 ms on 4 cores, about 20–90 ms on the Manta: about 80% of the wait after lift.
- Per pixel on one core it costs:
  - 9 ns on a blank page;
  - 17–20 ns when the brush lays permanent paint (Erasable below 1: the share rule's arithmetic);
  - 2.4 ns of that is `StrokeTones` gathering the rows; the rest is the one write rule.
- Estimated Manta wait from lift to page final, emulated:

  | Presets | Wait |
  |---|---|
  | Soft wash and Grey wash | 26–72 ms |
  | Wet hake / Big sky | 41–114 ms |
  | Hog bristle | 47–69 ms |
  | Charcoal | 44–66 ms |

  The device measured 0.05–0.25 s for washes and oils on the "wait after lift" branch, then the panel's own about
  0.3 s.

**Slow (Drying) washes** are live strokes, and the lift does more work:
- Slow bloom: about 0.13–0.24 s on the Manta, mostly the final compose.
- Slow ink wash: 0.29–0.34 s, mostly `BodySettle`'s blend and blur.
- While the pen is down, the preview's compose was most of the UI thread's work (fix 1).

So the user's wait is mostly the panel. The engine part that is left is the page write at lift, the Drying lift's
settle and compose, and contention from the save after every stroke. That last one is the likeliest cause of
"sometimes gets sluggish … (autosave?)" in the owner's notes of 2026-10-18.

## Ranked findings

The status of each finding is one of these:
- **done** on this branch;
- **deferred**: the area is changing on another branch;
- **owner**: it changes pixels or behaviour, so it is the owner's call;
- **device**: it needs a Manta measurement first.

### Done on this branch

1. **A Slow wash's preview recomposes only where its body newly lies** (`ModularCompose`, `83107b9`). Done.
   - **Before:** a transparent Drying body previews as a flat light grey (`previewWash`) that doesn't depend on how
     dark the body is. Yet every dab under the pen recomposed its whole footprint to write the same tones again.
   - **After:** in that preview only, a body counts as changed when it starts or stops lying at a pixel.
   - **Desktop, one core, the pen's thread for the bench stroke:**
     - Slow bloom 240: 45.6 → 27.3 ms (compose 41.9 → 24.4);
     - Slow bloom 120: 18.5 → 14.0 ms;
     - Slow ink wash 16: 21.5 → 17.2 ms.
   - **Desktop, 4 cores, per sample (mean / p95):**

     | Brush | Before | After |
     |---|---|---|
     | Watercolor Slow 360 | 0.05 / 0.50 ms | 0.03 / 0.36 ms |
     | Slow ink wash 240 | 0.11 / 0.66 ms | 0.09 / 0.43 ms |

   - **Manta:** about 10 → 7 ms at the 95th percentile per sample. The pen leaves 2.1 ms per sample, so this is the
     pen's lag on big Slow washes.
   - **Risk:** low; exact. Values too small to stay above 0 when averaged on the lattice are compared exactly.

2. **The save thread at the lowest priority** (`DocumentStore`, `dbb5060`). Done, needs a device check.
   - **Before:** after every stroke the recovery save compresses the whole page (`BookCodec`/`DocumentCodec` deflate):
     about 0.6 s of one desktop core for a typical two-pair page (the launch audit's measurement), so about 3–6 s on
     the Manta. It ran at the usual priority, so it took a core from the next stroke's helpers and the UI thread.
     When strokes come faster than that, it runs all the time.
   - **After:** it runs at the lowest priority, like the quick render, and takes what they leave.
   - **Gain:** for live strokes, the 3 helpers and the UI thread get all 4 cores back, so multi-core work is up to
     about 25% faster while a save runs. Quick renders already ran at the lowest priority, so for them the save now
     shares rather than wins.
   - **Risk:** low; saves take longer while the pen is busy. Desktop JVMs ignore thread priorities, so this needs a
     Manta measurement (the bench build, two strokes 1 s apart).

3. **The whole page renders on the cores, without a snapshot** (`DrawingPad.renderAll`, `7249a35`). Done, needs a
   device check.
   - **Before:** opening a drawing, turning a page, rotating or resizing rendered through the snapshot path: one core.
     The snapshot also marked every layer shared, so the next stroke's first write cloned whole layers on the UI
     thread: tones and coverage, about 10 MB; 15 MB with Erasable's shares.
   - **After:** it uses the area path a changed area already takes (`BrushTestPad` renders this way already).
   - **Desktop:** the native full-page compose is 5.7 ms on one core with 4 layers (3.1 ms with 2), about a third of
     that in 4 bands.
   - **Manta:** about 30–60 ms → 10–20 ms per page shown, and no 10–15 MB copy (about 10–16 ms, plus garbage) at the
     first stroke after it.
   - **Risk:** low; the same per-pixel compose (`compose_check`).

4. **The charcoal grain is built 4× faster** (`StickGrain`, `1215ade`). Done.
   - **Before:** the noise worked out each column's lattice cells and easing on every row, and eased each lattice row
     again for every pixel row.
   - **After:** both are worked out once.
   - **Desktop:** 2.33 → 0.54 ms per grain, with a CRC of every float unchanged.
   - **Manta:** about 47 → 11 ms. The grain is built at a charcoal or pastel pen-down when the brush isn't one of the
     two kept (more than two stick brushes in the slots), and for each stick sample in Brush Lab and the Tools page.
   - **Risk:** none; the same floats.

5. **Clean-up** (`33471e2`). Done.
   - `PressureStroke` is upstream's again.
   - `ToolSettings.pressureFor` is removed.
   - Dead methods are removed: `FloatBath.contours`, `lastEmpty` and `countPoints`; `HaloGrowth`'s `flags`;
     `LabUi.verticalRule`.
   - The four copies of "rethrow another thread's failure" are now `Cores.rethrow`.
   - `ToolsLayout` uses `LabLayout.clamp`.
   - 45 lines out, 15 in; INTEGRATION is updated.

### Deferred: the notebooks' areas (`PaintActivity` file flows, `DrawingBook`, `BookCodec`, `DocumentCodec`, `InkWashBar`)

These were found on `40adf69`, before the notebooks merged (`7bb0b1d`). Check each against the merged code before acting on it.

6. **Save less while drawing.** Done on `inkwash-saveless` (2026-10-19), needs a device check; see "Saving less, done"
   below. This was the biggest system-level win left.
   - **Was:** `PaintActivity.recovery()` (`DrawingPad.drawn` after every stroke, settle and dry) takes a snapshot and
     queues a full deflate of the page.
   - **Proposal:**
     - Hold the recovery save until about 2–3 s after the last lift, never while the pen is down or a wash dries.
       Pause and leaving should still save at once.
     - Use the fastest deflate level for the recovery file only: 630 → 175 ms on the desktop for 6% more bytes.
       Named files stay byte for byte.
     - Hand the packed bytes to the 60 s named autosave and to `DrawingBook.retainCurrent`, so neither compresses the
       same page again.
   - **Gain:** about one core back for the whole drawing session, plus battery. Page turns after an edit (now 661 ms
     desktop, so seconds on the Manta, on the UI thread in `DrawingBook.encode`) would become decode-only.
   - **Risk:** low–medium; a crash could lose up to about 3 s of strokes.

7. **The 10–15 MB layer clone after every recovery snapshot.** Done on `inkwash-saveless` (2026-10-19), the counter
   per layer below; see "Saving less, done".
   - **Was:** `ToneDocument.Layer.copy()` marks the layer shared, and the next stroke's first write clones the whole
     arrays (`writable()`). With a snapshot after every stroke, that is every stroke: about 10–16 ms at pen-down on
     the Manta, and 10–15 MB of garbage per stroke.
   - **Proposal:**
     - A snapshot released when its save is done or dropped clears `shared`, if the layer still holds the same arrays.
       This needs a counter per layer, checked under a lock in `writable()`. Snapshots nobody releases stay
       conservative.
     - Or tile-level copy-on-write, a bigger edit to upstream's `ToneDocument`.
   - With finding 6 it matters far less. It touches `DrawingBook.Snapshot` and the magnets' new `Snapshot` fields, so
     it should come after the notebooks branch merges.

8. **Opening a notebook inflates every page to validate it** (`BookCodec`, `DocumentCodec.validate`). Deferred.
   - About 70 ms per page on the desktop, about 1.4 s for 5 pages on the Manta.
   - The notebooks merge (`321f46c`) says notebooks now open "without inflating every page". This one is probably
     done already.

9. **Slot tiles drawn on the UI thread at launch** (`InkWashBar` constructor and `fit`, `now=true`). Deferred.
   - 7 tiles, then 7 again at the measured size: 90 ms cold on the desktop, 461 ms interpreted, so about 0.1–0.5 s
     before the first frame on the Manta.
   - **Proposal:** queue them on `SampleQueue`, showing the old bitmap scaled meanwhile.

10. **Layer thumbnails** (`InkWashBar`, 800 ms after the page goes still). Deferred.
    - They are cheap (about 20–30 ms on the Manta), but every pair's thumbnail is its own e-ink update.
    - **Proposal:** redraw only the pair that changed: fewer flashes.

### Saving less, done (findings 6 and 7, branch `inkwash-saveless`)

Checked against the merged notebooks code (`ae1fb5c`): the recovery save still ran after every stroke, settle and dry
(`DrawingPad.drawn`), packed the whole page at the default level, and a page turn after an edit packed it again on the
UI thread. Every suggestion was verified before it was used:

- **The save waits for a pause** (`RecoverySchedule`): 2.5 s after the last change, starting again at each; while changes
  keep coming, once the oldest is 5 s old; never while a stroke, shape, fill or gradient is down or finishing (looked
  at again every 250 ms); wet paint waits until the 5 s are up. A pause, a page turn, add or delete, a notebook opened or
  new, a named save and a rename save at once, as before; the notebook view and the shelf save a change still waiting.
- **A quick deflate** (`DocumentCodec.QUICK`, level 1) for what only memory and the recovery file keep. On the owner's 8
  heaviest pages (the dry run's Loose pages): 240 → 74 ms, 2.2 → 2.5 MB. Levels 2 and 3 were 76 and 92 ms for 15% and
  11% more bytes than the default, against 18%. Files stay at the default level, byte for byte (re-packing a stored page at
  the default level gives its bytes exactly, checked on 12 pages).
- **Packed once per change:** the recovery's packed page goes back to the book, so a page turn after it packs nothing,
  and a turn right after drawing packs at `QUICK` (240 → 74 ms on the UI thread). A named save packs each quick page at
  the default level on the file thread, then the book reads them from the file (`savedPages`); the recovery after a
  named save, which packed the page again (240 ms), now names it in the file (0.05 ms). One trade: a named save packs again each
  page packed quickly (241 → 281 ms here for the drawn page and one turned from), on the file thread, not the UI's.
- **The layer copy:** a snapshot released once written or dropped takes its count back from each layer; at 0 the next
  write writes in place. With the save at pauses, the next stroke copies nothing when the save is done before it starts
  (74 ms here, about 1.5 s on the Manta); otherwise it copies once, as before.

| Per stroke, desktop (Manta at most ×20) | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| UI thread: snapshot + the layer copy at the next stroke | 0.58 ms (12 ms), and 9–14 MB of garbage | none (a timer reset) |
| File thread: the recovery save | 240 ms (4.8 s) | none; 74 ms (1.5 s) per pause |
| UI thread: a page turn right after drawing | 240 ms (4.8 s) | 74 ms (1.5 s); none after a pause |
| File thread: the recovery after a named save | 240 ms (4.8 s) | 0.05 ms |

Simulated on the Manta's timings (`SaveLessChecks`, saves 20× this Mac's): 30 strokes in bursts of five with 4 s looks,
6 saves (were 13 written of 30 asked, each 4.7 s: the file thread never stopped); 60 strokes nonstop, 10 (were 14). A
crash at any 10 ms restores the page as after a finished stroke and loses at most 6.4 s of strokes (was 9.3 s, the saves
then being 4.7 s each): 5 s of waiting, a stroke down then, and the save's own time.

For the device: the bench build, draw for a minute (bursts and nonstop) and watch for the sluggishness; turn a page right
after drawing and after a pause; kill the app from the recent apps 3 s and 10 s after a stroke and reopen it.

### Needs a device test or an owner decision (Brush Lab and the Tools page blinking)

11. **Every popup's close forces a clean refresh of the whole test page.** Device.
    - **Where:** `BrushTestPad.setCovered(false)` → `renderAll` marks `unrefreshed`, then `connect` → `refreshSoon` →
      a full gray refresh of about half the screen after 300 ms.
    - **When:** after every choice panel, the size/grey picker, Load and rename. The focus change does it too.
    - This is the biggest blink in Brush Lab (about 270 ms of panel time and a visible flash).
    - **Proposal:** refresh only the area the popup covered (`SwatchFaces.Host.covered` has it), or skip it when
      nothing was drawn while covered.
    - **Risk:** popup ghosting if the refresh is skipped. Try it on the Manta first.

12. **Samples come back in several e-ink updates** (`SampleQueue.HOLD_MS` 400). Device.
    - With slow brushes, each sample past 400 ms is its own update: up to 4 per slider release, about 10 for a choice
      in Advanced.
    - **Proposal:** hand back everything one change asked for together, capped at about 1.5 s.
    - **Trade-off:** the first sample shows a little later.

13. **Cards draw blank, then fill in, even when their sample is cached** (`LoopCard.request`; library pages,
    variations, choice tiles, the Tools page's tabs). Device.
    - **Proposal:** check `WildSample`'s cache synchronously before the first draw, keep the picture of the two loops
      that are kept and moved, and reuse card views instead of `removeAllViews`.
    - **Gain:** a cached tab or page turn goes from 2–4 updates to 1.

14. **Advanced rebuilds whole blocks for small taps** (`LabSettings` 90–151, 377–424).
    - Opening a part rebuilds the column (about 60 views). A hand-grid cell, a dial segment or a lock rebuilds the
      open card (about 40 views).
    - **Proposal:** update cells and segments in place, and keep the rebuild key for structure only.
    - **Gain:** a small area updates instead of a quarter of the screen.
    - **Risk:** low, but it is UI work best checked on the device.

15. **The brush's loop is drawn twice per change**, once in the bar and once in "This brush" (`TestArea` 106–107,
    `BrushLabActivity` 234). Owner.
    - DESIGN.md says the loop appears once, not again in a top bar.
    - Dropping the bar's loop saves a render and an update per change, but it is a visible change.

### Looked at and left (exact speed-ups that didn't pay)

- **The page write's division by coverage, done by a reciprocal multiply instead.** It is exact over every input,
  but it gave no gain on the desktop's ARM; on the Manta's A55 it might, so it is a candidate for a device A/B.
  Otherwise the write rule is about 25 cycles a pixel and branchy. A specialised path for "layer as found, Erasable
  1" could reach about 2× but duplicates the one write rule in upstream's `ToneDocument`. Not worth the risk without
  device numbers.
- **PaperNoise.** Hoisting its column math gave warm 3.0 → 1.7 ms but no change cold (13 ms) or interpreted (83 ms),
  which is what launch pays. Reverted. Building it on a background thread at launch would help only if nothing needs
  it at once, and the slot tiles do (finding 9).
- **The stampers' hot loops** (`HakeRibbon.Lay.head`, `BristleSwarm.hair`, `LineStamper.dab`) already skip what they
  can.
  - A conservative row span for `BristleSwarm.hair`'s swept capsule would save about 20% of its pixels' square roots,
    about 6% of a Hog bristle render. Small.
  - Reciprocals in place of their per-pixel divisions would change rounding.
- **`BodySettle.blend`'s line pull** is serial by design: a halo laid changes what later pixels read. Its
  `FloatTiles.get/set` neighbourhood loop could read tiles once per row: an exact speed-up of a part that is about
  10% of a Slow ink wash lift.
- **Magnets** (merged at `40adf69`):
  - Tile pictures are cached (160); the canvas draws them only where an area renders, and nothing when a page has
    none.
  - `renderDirty` copies `takeMagnetAreas()` into a new list on every present, a negligible allocation.
  - Pictures evicted from the cache aren't recycled. That is harmless (the garbage collector frees them), but worth
    a `recycle` in `removeEldestEntry` if the bin's churn ever shows in memory.
- **Brush names** (merged at `f34fed5`): names are rolled once per brush and drawing, so they cost nothing per stroke.
