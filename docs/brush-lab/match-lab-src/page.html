      Grain

      Same format as the app's papers: 256 by 256 grey, 8 bits, mean 128, spread 52. The .bin is the raw file the app keeps in assets/textures; the PNG holds the same pixels.

      On four brushes

      How it works

      1. Photo. The page evens out the lighting, then reads the bare paper around the stroke: its tone and its texture. The paper becomes white, and black ink that photographed grey is darkened back to black. Faint flecks on the paper are dropped, so they can't pass for a halo or a wash.
      2. Measure. The stroke is scaled to about 440 px long, as drawn on the Manta, and thinned to its centre line. Along that line the page measures width, tapers, the shape of each end (square, round or pointed), edge roughness, tone, dry-brush gaps, any halo past the edge, splatter, parallel lines and grain.
      3. Scan. Trial brushes are painted along the photo's own centre line, with pressure taken from its widths, and compared with the photo. The closest few get their dials tuned (Nelder–Mead), then each paper is tried if grain shows. About five seconds; about three with a tip or a category chosen first. A chosen tip is the only one tried; a category's tips and media are tried first and favoured. A ragged edge and a little wobble are smoothed out before measuring, so only a plainly rough edge, like charcoal's or crayon's, reads as rough.
      4. Drawn tools. The app's drawn tools (the tool picker's, or the chosen category's) are each painted along the same line at a few sizes; the closest are named, to show which drawing a scan could stand in for.
      5. Pencil case. Name each scan and add it. The set becomes one import file of named share codes, kept in this browser until cleared.
      6. Paper. The lighting is evened out, the grain is scaled to the app's papers, broad blotches are removed, and the edges are blended so the tile repeats without a seam.
